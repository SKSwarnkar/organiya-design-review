Image creation and approval workflow

  1. Reference pack: product owner maps actual snack photos, pack artwork and label versions to each SKU; content owner confirms usage rights. Preserve originals in a private asset archive.
  2. Art direction: cream stone, matte bowls, muted green linen, restrained mango accents and natural light. Match food scale, texture and ingredients. Preserve the full bowl or pouch in 4:5 product views; use 3:2 for editorial scenes.
  3. Generate selectively: reference the approved snack photographs and O’s approved visual style. Produce two candidate compositions for each missing scene, then refine the chosen one. Avoid invented package art, written claims or logos. Keep family imagery optional.
  4. Product review: compare output and source side by side. Check food shape, ingredients, portion presentation, anatomy if people appear, brand consistency and unintended text. Reject images that depict a different SKU.
  5. Asset record: save source reference, prompt, tool/model when known, generation date, original output, editor changes, rights/consent, approver, approval date, label version and checksum. Mark provenance unknown where it is unknown.
  6. Delivery: use 1200 × 1500 or larger source images where available; current six review derivatives are 1000 × 1250. Do not upscale merely to meet a number. Produce 320/640/960/1200 responsive variants when the original supports them, WebP/AVIF with fallback, intrinsic dimensions and full-size label files.
  7. Publish: import only approved assets into the Organiya CMS. Retain a clear illustrative caption for generated food/lifestyle scenes. Keep alt text useful, with no unsubstantiated health claims. Validate every gallery and card on mobile.

Effort: allow 1–2 working days for image selection/generation, optimization and one revision round after references are approved, parallel with development. Family imagery can add 0.5–1 day. A photography shoot, product reformulation or label approval is a separate dependency.

Practical image rules

  • Use a stable 4:5 main stage and thumbnails. Preserve all pouch edges and label content with contain. A landscape pairing may have breathing room around it.
  • Suggested source minimum: 1200 × 1500 for product photography; preserve higher-resolution originals for label reading. Deliver responsive WebP/AVIF and retain a full-size label asset.
  • Every item has an ID, type, file, alt text, visible label, caption, order, approval state and source/rights record. Recommended 4–7 useful images; support any sensible number with wrapping thumbnails.
  • Missing or unapproved assets stay out of the public gallery. The current preview demonstrates three illustrative views per product; it does not substitute them for front/back/nutrition assets.
  • Do not bake nutritional text into lifestyle photos as the only source of information. Keep the approved facts as accessible, editable text too.

Protein and other claims

“X% protein” is ambiguous unless its basis is clear. Prefer an approved quantity and basis such as grams per 100 g or grams per stated serving; only use percentage, “high protein”, “source of”, “gluten-free”, “baked” or similar claims when the exact product and wording support them. Store the approved statement, basis, evidence reference, approving owner and label version. A formulation change should invalidate the approval.

Website advertising claims need to be supportable and consistent with the label. The launch content review should use the current FSSAI Advertising and Claims rules and amendments, with a qualified food-label reviewer confirming the product-specific wording. Later online selling also needs the applicable pre-sale label information; check the current Labelling and Display rules and amendments. This plan does not assume a blanket certification or regulatory clearance.

Approval and publishing rules

A product progresses through draft → product review → approved → published. Media has its own status, so replacing a front/back label or the formulation reopens approval on the relevant claims and images. Keep theme/layout changes developer-controlled; ordinary copy and image changes belong in the editor. For a small team, use a documented founder release check if the chosen CMS tier does not enforce separate publisher roles.

Use a protected preview for unpublished material; noindex alone is not access control. Preview builds can use server-side build credentials to fetch drafts, but those tokens must not ship to visitors. Public production builds query approved, published content only. Deploy-hook URLs remain secret and repeated publishes should be grouped to avoid unnecessary rebuilds.

Editor acceptance workflow

  1. Editor opens a product or story document, changes text or uploads an image, and saves a draft.
  2. Preview displays the draft in the O layout. Product cards, detail pages and related-product links all use the same record.
  3. Founder reviews labels, claims and image rights. Editors cannot publish a missing required field or a broken product reference.
  4. Publish triggers one validated build. On failure, keep the last good public deployment and notify the operator; never replace missing CMS data with synthetic fallback products.
  5. Show the published revision and time. Keep previous content versions and periodic exports so a content error can be rolled back.

Required handover exercise: Organiya changes a story paragraph, replaces one card image, reorders a gallery and updates a serving idea without changing code. Verify both preview and production, then practise restoring the previous version.

06 · Real inquiry delivery

Use the designed O forms with a managed form endpoint. The current preview continues to send nothing. Production delivery is a separate integration and must be verified before DNS cutover.

  • Consumer fields: name, email, selected product, optional city and message. Business inquiries add business name, type and city/region; phone remains optional.
  • Context: submit stable product ID/name, page URL and inquiry type. Allow a general-range question in production with matching page context.
  • Routing: one confirmed Organiya inbox, a named backup owner and a visible acknowledgement. A submission is successful only after the provider accepts it; preserve entered text on failure.
  • Abuse controls: provider-side spam filtering, honeypot/rate controls, length limits and no file uploads at launch. Domain restrictions and CAPTCHA are enabled where supported by the chosen plan.
  • Data handling: linked privacy notice; explain purpose, provider and retention. Proposed operational retention is 90 days for closed inquiries, subject to owner/legal confirmation. No marketing opt-in bundled into a product inquiry.
  • Operations: proposed response target of two working days, only published after the team accepts it. Check the dashboard if email fails; escalate unattended inquiries to the backup owner.
  • Acceptance: consumer and distributor messages arrive in the real inbox/dashboard, with correct SKU context; test invalid email, spam, duplicate click, quota exhaustion and provider failure. The operator acknowledges the test receipt.

The legacy Organiya source contains Google Forms embeds and an email-setup guide. Their ownership, current fields and delivery have not been verified in this task. Reuse requires that verification; do not assume an existing embed means inquiries are monitored.

Rollback

Trigger rollback immediately for a serious content/claim error or data exposure; for a broken critical route, failed TLS or failed inquiry delivery, use a pre-agreed 15-minute troubleshooting limit. The operator can roll back within the approved launch window without waiting for a second meeting.

  1. New-site code/content issue: pause publishes and restore a known-good production Pages deployment. Restore the matching CMS revision before the next build so the fault does not return. A preview deployment is not a Pages rollback target; the first launch must retain the legacy fallback if no earlier good production deployment exists. Pages rollback procedure.
  2. Domain/TLS or first-release failure: restore the four apex GitHub A records and the www → skswarnkar.github.io CNAME from the snapshot, including prior TTL/proxy state. Remove the conflicting Pages apex CNAME. Pause new redirect rules only where they conflict with the restored host. Keep the legacy GitHub domain binding and HTTPS setting intact.
  3. Verify recovery: check apex/www/HTTPS, representative legacy routes, contact access and email routing on desktop and mobile data. Allow cached DNS to expire; confirm from multiple resolvers. Record the incident, restored release and follow-up fix before attempting another cutover.

Launch record to retain: source branch and commit, CMS snapshot, image approval version, build result, Pages deployment ID/URL, zone export and exact DNS diff, TLS status, redirect results, inbox acceptance, founder approval, rollback target and operator. Keep credentials and mail-recipient addresses in the secure operational record.

09 · Launch acceptance

  • Visual: O’s approved palette/cards and the new announcement match; original logos are legible; 320, 390, 768 and 1440px layouts have no overflow; check Chrome, Safari and a real Android/iPhone.
  • Catalog: only approved snacks are listed; filters and ingredient/name search combine correctly; empty and cleared states work; every card has a valid detail URL.
  • Gallery: every published image loads; front/back/nutrition labels retain proportions; keyboard activation, Escape and focus return work; mobile full-size reading is usable; missing assets are hidden.
  • Image approval: all nine current illustrations have an explicit keep/replace decision; every published image matches its SKU, has alt text and provenance, and no unapproved pack/claim artwork appears.
  • Product facts: approved SKU names, ingredients, allergens, nutrition basis, packaging and claims agree. No placeholder percentages, invented reviews, certifications, prices or unsupported availability.
  • Inquiries: real delivery confirmed by the owner, correct product context, validation, honest success/failure, privacy notice and monitored response responsibility.
  • Editing: draft/save/preview/publish/rollback proven. No content change requires a component edit for the agreed editable fields.
  • SEO: real static routes; unique title/description, canonical links, image alt, sitemap and useful redirects. Organization/breadcrumb metadata only from approved facts; no invented Product Offers or ratings.
  • Accessibility: semantic headings/labels, visible focus, keyboard menus/gallery, readable contrast and reduced-motion support. Nutrition information has a text equivalent.
  • Performance: size responsive images; lazy-load below-fold media; avoid fetching every full-resolution gallery image upfront. Aim for LCP ≤2.5s and CLS ≤0.1 in representative tests, then validate real-world performance after traffic exists.
  • Operations: access ownership recorded, no secrets in frontend/repository, backup/export restored once, error and quota alerts routed, rollback tested.

Go/no-go: the founder approves the exact production preview, product/label owner clears the published facts and claims, and the inbox owner confirms receipt of test inquiries. Record pass/fail evidence by release, with no unresolved critical findings. A product with unresolved evidence stays draft or loses the unverified claim; it does not block unrelated approved snacks.
